What it does

One short list, every Monday.

Alfredo reads the sales you already ring through your existing POS system, then looks at your menu, your guests and the places down the street. Every Monday it turns that into three to five changes worth trying. You choose which ones to run as a test. It reports back.

Inside a suggestion

What a suggestion tells you before you decide.

Every one of them comes with the same six things, so you can judge it in a few seconds and get back to the floor.

  1. 01

    What it is. The change itself, in one line you could read out to the floor.

  2. 02

    Why it came up. What in your own sales put it on the list this week.

  3. 03

    How much work. Low, medium or high, so a busy week gets the easy ones.

  4. 04

    What it risks. What could go the other way, said plainly before you try it.

  5. 05

    How to do it. The steps, and who needs to know.

  6. 06

    When to check back. The date you choose. Alfredo suggests one, and you can change it.

Beyond the list

Your menu, your guests, and what is happening nearby.

What every plate really earns.

Once the extras are counted, the plate that sells most is often not the plate that earns most. Alfredo separates the two from your own recipes and ingredient costs, shows which items are selling below what they cost to make, names the one ingredient squeezing a plate, tells a real cost problem apart from a false alarm, and says which items to show off, keep, or rethink.

Who comes in, and what is happening nearby.

New faces, the regulars, and the ones who have gone quiet, as far as your POS tells you who is who. Then the places down the street: what they list publicly, set beside your own prices, who is open later than you by the hours they list, and what reviews in your area keep saying. Alongside that, your sales by day and time of day, your locations side by side on the Group plan, and the drinks you make against the brands you stock.
