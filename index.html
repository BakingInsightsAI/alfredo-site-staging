A week with it

One Monday, and the four weeks after.

The same five moments every week, in your own sales. You decide at every one of them.

  1. Monday morning

    The question

    Last week felt busy and the takings did not show it. You cannot say why, and you have a service to run.

  2. Still Monday morning

    The discovery

    Alfredo has already been through the week's sales and found what is behind it.

  3. Before service

    The choice

    A short list of small changes worth trying, each one easy to undo. You pick the ones that suit your place.

  4. The weeks after

    The test

    You make the change. Alfredo watches the weeks that follow and says nothing until it knows.

  5. Four weeks on

    The answer

    It worked, it did nothing, or it backfired. You find out which, and decide what comes next.

What lands every week

Not all one kind of change.

A suggestion can come from any corner of your sales. Each one says what it is, why it came up, how much work it is, what it risks, and when to check back.

  • Changes to try, not charts to read.
  • Every one of them comes out of your own sales.
  • Every change ends in a verdict: worked, no effect, or backfired.
  • Alfredo never changes a menu or a price on its own.

Finished, with the answer

Illustrative example

  • Worked

    House lager: $247 more a week

    Raised fifty cents. Four weeks on, the same number of pints sold. After what you would have made anyway.

  • No effect

    Calamari moved to the top of the starters

    Four weeks on, it sold the same. Alfredo said so, and the top spot went to something that does move.

  • Backfired

    Happy hour pushed an hour later

    The early crowd did not come back and the later hour did not fill. It went back the same night.

What Alfredo looks at

Your own sales, read the way an owner would read them.

  • Your menu

    What each plate really earns once the extras are counted, and which items to show off, keep or rethink.

  • Your guests

    New faces, regulars, and the ones who have gone quiet and are worth winning back.

  • Your costs

    Which ingredients went up, which plates they are squeezing, and what to do about it.

  • The neighborhood

    What nearby places charge, when they are open, and what their guests keep saying.

  • Your busy times

    Which days and hours carry the week, and which ones are quietly costing you.

  • Your taproom

    House pours against guest pours, which beers earn their line, and what to put beside them.

Who it is for

Independent places, and small groups of them.
