The story

From a question you cannot answer to one you can.

Five stages, every week, in your own sales. You decide at every one of them.

  1. One

    The question

    Something in the week feels off, or feels good, and you cannot say why.

  2. Two

    The discovery

    Alfredo goes through your own sales and finds what is behind it.

  3. Three

    The choice

    You get a short list of small changes worth trying. You pick the ones you want.

  4. Four

    The test

    You make the change. Alfredo watches the weeks that follow.

  5. Five

    The answer

    It worked, it did nothing, or it backfired. Alfredo tells you which.

What lands every week

Three to five things worth trying. Never a wall of charts.

Each one says what it is, why it came up, how much work it is, what it risks, and when to check back. Nothing changes unless you choose it.

Finished, with the answer

  • Worked

    House lager up fifty cents

    Four weeks on, the bar took more and sold the same number of pints. After what you would have made anyway.

  • No effect

    Dessert added to the set menu

    Three weeks on, nothing moved either way. Alfredo said so rather than finding a number to show.

  • Backfired

    Happy hour pushed an hour later

    The early crowd did not come back and the later hour did not fill. It went back the same night.

What Alfredo looks at

Your own sales, read the way an owner would read them.

  • Your menu

    What each plate really earns once the extras are counted, and which items to show off, keep or rethink.

  • Your guests

    New faces, regulars, and the ones who have gone quiet and are worth winning back.

  • Your costs

    Which ingredients went up, which plates they are squeezing, and what to do about it.

  • The neighborhood

    What nearby places charge, when they are open, and what their guests keep saying.

  • Your busy times

    Which days and hours carry the week, and which ones are quietly costing you.

  • Your taproom

    House pours against guest pours, which beers earn their line, and what to put beside them.

Who it is for

Independent places, and small groups of them.

Why it is different

Most tools hand you charts. This one hands you a short list.

  • Changes to try, not charts to read

    A dashboard shows you what happened. Alfredo tells you what to do about it, in the words you would use yourself.

  • Your own sales, nobody else's

    Every suggestion comes out of what you already ring through your existing POS system.

  • A verdict on every change

    Worked, no effect, or backfired. Every test ends on one of those three words.

  • Honest when it is not working

    If a change did nothing, Alfredo says it did nothing. If the data is not there, it says pending.

  • You stay in control

    Alfredo never changes a menu or a price on its own. It suggests, and you decide.
